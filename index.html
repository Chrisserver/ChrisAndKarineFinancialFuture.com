    Where each dollar goes

    1. Until the wedding: 50-60% of surplus to house, wedding and honeymoon cash, 40-50% to investing (at least $500/month)
    2. Both of you open a CELIAPP: deductible, tax-free for the house
    3. After the wedding: house first, still $500-1,000/month invested
    4. After the house: 15-20% of combined gross income to long-term wealth (CELI, REER, employer plans)
    Money you need within about 2 years belongs in high-interest savings or GICs, not stocks. And never spend every dollar on the house: a $475k house with a $30k emergency fund beats a $500k house with $3k left.

    Income sources in retirement
    • REER, then converted to a FERR (minimum withdrawals from age 71)
    • CELI (tax-free withdrawals)
    • RRQ (Québec pension) and OAS (SV), plus GIS (SRG) if income is low
    • Employer plan: pension (RRPD/RRCD) or RVER
    • Non-registered investments and annuities
    • Home equity: downsize or reverse mortgage
    Limits for 2026
    • REER: 18% of last year's earnings, up to $33,810 (unused room carries over)
    • CELI: $7,000/yr, or up to $109,000 total if never used since 2009
    • REER withdrawal tax withheld in Québec: 19%, 24%, 29% by amount

    Québec and federal accounts

    CELIAPP (first home)Home
    • $8,000/yr, $40,000 lifetime each, so up to $80,000 for two. Deductible on contribution
    • Withdrawals for a first home are tax-free
    • Room only starts once the account is open, so open one soon, even with a small deposit
    • Unused room carries forward, up to $8,000
    REER and the Home Buyers' Plan (RAP)HomeRetirement
    • Withdraw up to $60,000 from your REER for a first home
    • Can be combined with the CELIAPP on the same purchase
    CELIWedding

    $7,000/yr, tax-free growth and withdrawals. Ideal for the wedding and honeymoon money, since withdrawing does not tax you.

    Reductions and benefits to check

    Home
    • Federal Home Buyers' Amount: a $10,000 amount worth up to $1,500 in tax credit
    • Québec has no first-time exemption on the "welcome tax", so budget it
    • Insured mortgages can reach a 30-year amortization for first-time buyers, which lowers monthly payments
    • Rénoclimat: Québec grants for energy upgrades after an audit
    Marriage
    • In Québec, spouses can transfer unused tax credits to each other
    • File taxes together each year and compare with filing separately
    Children
    • Québec Family Allowance (up to $3,068/child, 2026) and Canada Child Benefit (up to $8,157/yr under 6)
    • RQAP: Québec parental insurance for maternity, paternity and parental leave
    • Subsidized daycare at reduced contribution, or a tax credit for private childcare
    • REEE (education savings): federal grant of 20% up to $500/yr (lifetime $7,200), plus Québec's 10% incentive
    Retirement and everyday income
    • Fonds de solidarité FTQ and Fondaction: combined federal and Québec credit of about 30%, on up to $5,000 (confirm current rates)
    • Ask your employer about RVER or a matching contribution
    • Solidarity tax credit and GST credit: already in your budget grid, claimed automatically when you file
    • Always file your tax return, even on low income, to receive these credits
    Rules change often. Check amounts on Revenu Québec and Canada Revenue Agency pages, or with an accountant, before you rely on them.
